How to play Excerptle

You see the first sentence of a book. Name the book in six guesses. Everything else on this page is detail.

The sentence

Each puzzle opens with a single sentence and nothing around it — no author, no year, no cover, no genre. That is deliberate. A first line is the most rewritten sentence in a novel, and it is usually the only part of the book you can recognise cold.

Everyone in the world gets the same daily puzzle, and it rolls over at midnight UTC. If you would rather not wait, the book bank holds 600 more you can play in any order.

Guessing

Type a title and press Guess. You get six. A wrong guess costs you one of the six and nothing else — it never reveals more text, so there is no penalty for thinking out loud early.

  • Close spelling counts. “Pride and Predjudice” is accepted. So is dropping a leading “The”.
  • Partial titles get a nudge. If your guess contains a distinctive word from the title, you are told how many key title words are still missing — never which ones.
  • One vague word is not a guess. Something like “great” will not solve the book or earn you that nudge. Commit to more of the title.

Hints

Stuck is normal. A hint does not tell you the answer; it shows you more of the book. There are five, and they open the text outward:

StartThe first sentence
Hint 1The first paragraph
Hint 2The first few paragraphs
Hint 3The first couple of pages
Hint 4The whole first chapter
Hint 5The author’s name

Only the last one names a person. The other four are just more of the same prose, which means a hint is really an invitation to keep reading — often you solve it a paragraph before you meant to.

Scoring and streaks

The fewer hints and guesses you spend, the better you place. The leaderboard brackets players by how many hints they used, so a clean solve is never beaten by someone who read the whole chapter first. Winning the daily puzzle extends your streak; missing a day resets it.

Progress, streaks and settings live in your own browser. Signing in is optional and exists only so those numbers can follow you to another device.

The three ways to play

  • Daily puzzle — one book, everyone, midnight UTC.
  • Book bank — 600 books at your own pace, in any order.
  • Battle — two browsers, the same book, first correct title wins.
