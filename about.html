About Excerptle

Excerptle is a daily guessing game built on the opening lines of public-domain literature. You get one sentence, six guesses, and five optional hints: an opening excerpt, genre, original publication year, setting, and author.

Why openings

A first sentence is the most deliberate line in a novel. It is the one an author rewrites last, and the one readers quote back decades later. Most of us carry a few of them around without ever having decided to memorise them — which makes them a good thing to guess at, and a better thing to be reminded of.

So Excerptle starts you with a single sentence and nothing else. No author, no year, no cover. If it lands, you will know it immediately. If it does not, the opening excerpt and factual hints give you other ways to recognise it.

Where the books come from

Every text is drawn from Project Gutenberg, the archive of works whose copyright has expired. That means the canon here is mostly pre-1930: Austen, Melville, the Brontës, Conrad, Twain, Wells, plus a long tail of adventure serials and forgotten bestsellers that were enormous in their day.

Gutenberg publishes the same work many times over — different editions, multi-part splits, alternate transcriptions. Excerptle collapses those into one entry per book, so you will never be handed the same novel twice under two different numbers. At most one chapter of any book is hosted here; for the rest of it, go to Gutenberg, where it is free.

How the game is built

There are three ways to play. The daily puzzle is the same book for everyone in the world, rolling over at midnight Pacific time. The book bank is 600 books you can work through at your own pace, in any order. Battle mode connects two browsers directly and hands both players the same book — first correct title wins.

Your progress, streaks and settings live in your own browser. Signing in is optional, and exists so those numbers can follow you to another device.

Who makes it

Excerptle is made by Provable Learning LLC, an independent education studio. We also make VocabHelp, a vocabulary-learning tool for students and families.

Excerptle is free to play and intends to stay that way. It is supported by a single advertisement after a finished game, by tips, and by Excerptle Pro, which removes that advertisement for anyone who would rather pay than look at it.
